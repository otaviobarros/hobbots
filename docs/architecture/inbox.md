# A hob's backpack and continuations

**Decided** ([ADR 004](../decisions/004-durable-inbox.md), [ADR 019](../decisions/019-hob-backpack-and-continuations.md)). Each hob has a durable **backpack** of responsibilities, not merely a notification list. A **backpack item** is one thing the hob still has to act on.

A hob's backpack is not your Inbox. Your Inbox holds what needs you: approval requests and questions no lead could answer. A hob's backpack holds what needs that hob. Your answer to an escalated question becomes a backpack item for the hob that asked.

## Item kinds

Kinds are Task, result, failure, checkpoint (milestone, finding or blocker), question, answer, hand-off, message, approval outcome, deadline and progress. [ADR 019](../decisions/019-hob-backpack-and-continuations.md#item-kinds) says who makes each and who receives it.

Every kind except progress is **actionable** and can wake the hob. Progress, including the hub's started and waiting states, is **routine**. It updates the screens at once and reaches the hob as a digest in its next turn, but never wakes it on its own (IN-005). Deadline reminders support existing work; this does not introduce the deferred general recurring-automation product.

## Processing lifecycle

| State | Meaning | Durable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pending | Committed with the change that caused it, waiting for a turn | Item ID, recipient, kind, actionable or routine, Task and Quest IDs, correlation, dedupe key, payload, creation time, not-before time for deadlines. |
| Delivered | Put into a turn: a continuation's prompt, or a live turn's waiting tool call | Delivery attempt with its target, turn and runtime session. |
| Acknowledged | The runtime accepted the turn that carries it | The adapter's start evidence for that turn. |
| Processing | The turn is running and holds the item's claim | Claim owner, fencing token and lease expiry. |
| Resolved | Handled | Disposition: consumed by a turn, answered by a tool call, late, or dismissed. |
| Failed | Retries exhausted, or the recipient can no longer take it | Error, every attempt, and a blocker with its next action. |

Adapters and Hobbots services record real evidence. Inactivity is not acknowledgement. An item whose turn ends early goes back to Pending with a new attempt; it never counts as resolved.

## Records

The backpack is built in `@hobbots/inbox`, `@hobbots/persistence` and migration 0026 ([#290](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/290)), delivery in `@hobbots/inbox` ([#291](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/291)), continuations in `@hobbots/agents`, `@hobbots/inbox` and the hub ([#292](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/292)), and blockers in migration 0029, the hub and the web client ([#296](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/296)).

- **`BackpackItem`** (`@hobbots/domain`): recipient, kind, actionable, priority, state, the Task, Quest, Attempt and thread it belongs to, dedupe key, payload, not-before time, claim, fencing token, deliveries, disposition, error and blockers. Its `questId` is the nearest Quest above its Task, the one whose lead runs the work.
- **`backpackItemsFor`** (`@hobbots/inbox`): the pure rule that turns a change's journal events into items. The repositories call it inside every journaled change, so the hob tools need no change. Routine progress is recorded too; it is not actionable. The hub's own ending checkpoints make no item, because the result or failure says it.
- **`Backpack`** (`@hobbots/inbox`, `BackpackLive` in `@hobbots/persistence`): claim, renew, deliver, acknowledge, process, requeue, resolve, fail, abandon, decide, expire claims, and run an effect fenced by the claim, plus reads of a hob's, a Quest's and the workspace's open items and of the items whose blocker waits for you. Every step after the claim checks the owner, the token, the lease by the database's clock and the step's starting states in one conditional update. A refused step writes nothing.
- **Tables:** `backpack_items`, unique on recipient and dedupe key, holds the claim's owner, token and lease; `backpack_deliveries` keeps each delivery with the token it was made under and the runtime's acknowledgement; `backpack_blockers` keeps every time the item failed, why, its next action, how many deliveries it had used, and what you chose.
- **`Dispatcher`** (`@hobbots/inbox`): delivers one item into a turn the caller describes. It claims the item first, so an item already settled or claimed is never delivered again. It checks the item against its Task and resolves it as late without a turn when the Task it is checked against has ended or its recipient no longer owns it. Otherwise it records the delivery, moves the item to acknowledged and processing only on the session the runtime reported, renews the lease every third of it while the turn runs, and resolves the item as consumed when the turn ends. The turn commits each of the item's effects through the delivery, keyed by the item, in a transaction that first checks the claim still holds; the last effect can resolve the item in the same transaction. An effect whose claim was lost writes nothing. A claim's owner is the hub process and the turn. A turn that ends early, fails, dies or is interrupted sends the item back to pending, and it is retried with backoff; the third delivery since its last retry by default (`HOBBOTS_BACKPACK_MAX_DELIVERIES`) fails it instead, as a blocker. A turn whose claim is lost is stopped and its outcome dropped. A deadline is not delivered before its not-before time. At start the dispatcher expires every claim an earlier process held, then expires lapsed claims once per lease. Its Task trees and event publishing come from the hub through `DispatcherHost`.
- **`Dispatcher.dispatchAll`**: the same delivery for every item still waiting, put into one turn. The caller's plan sees the waiting items before any is claimed and returns the turn, or nothing when no turn can start now, as for a paused hob: then no item is claimed and each stays pending. A turn that ends early is planned again with backoff for the items it sent back to pending.
- **`carryingThread`**, **`continuationThread`** and **`arrivalsText`** (`@hobbots/inbox`): which thread's next turn carries an item, which thread's chat an actionable item continues, and what that turn is told of the items it carries, in Hobbots' own words, routine progress folded into one digest.
- **`Continuations`** (`@hobbots/agents`, `ContinuationsLive` in the hub): the dispatcher's continuation branch as the chat sees it: the threads where work waits for their hob, and the delivery of that work into a thread's next turn. `WakeOnArrival` wakes a thread when an actionable item for it is recorded or goes back to pending.
- **Events:** `BackpackItemRecorded` follows the change's own events in the same transaction, and `BackpackItemChanged` records every later step.

| Item kind | Made from | Recipient |
|---|---|---|
| Task | `TaskCreated` for a step or a delegated Task | The new Task's owner |
| Result | `TaskCompleted` | The parent Task's owner |
| Failure | `TaskStateChanged` to failed, blocked or cancelled | The parent Task's owner |
| Checkpoint | `CheckpointRecorded` for a milestone, finding, blocker or question | The nearest lead |
| Progress | `CheckpointRecorded` for progress, started or waiting | The nearest lead |
| Question | `QuestionAsked`, `QuestionEscalated` | The lead it is with; none once it reaches you |
| Answer | `QuestionAnswered` | The hob that asked |
| Hand-off | `HandedOff` | The receiving Task's owner |

An approval request that expires because Hobbots restarted, so no live turn waits for it, records an Approval outcome for the hob that asked, in its thread, with the expiry. Messages and deadlines have their kinds and payloads but no source yet. They come with the commands that make them.

## Delivery and recovery

1. Commit the item in the same transaction as the change that caused it. The backpack row is the outbox.
2. A dispatcher claims the item with a lease and a fencing token, then delivers it with stable identity and correlation. Delivery is at least once.
3. Record acknowledgement separately from delivery.
4. Deduplicate by item ID and dedupe key before applying any effect.
5. Persist the outcome and linked Task changes in one transaction, guarded by the fencing token.
6. Queue the next continuation or outgoing item durably; publish UI updates after commit.

Each item has a dedupe key, unique per recipient. Keys reuse the hob tools' idempotency keys, the source record's id and version, or your command's key. An outcome write checks the token, the claim's owner and the lease's expiry in one conditional update. Expiring a claim clears its owner and raises its token in one update, so a worker whose lease expired is refused at once, before any other worker claims the item. Retry transport failures with bounded backoff. For effects outside the database, use provider idempotency where available and reconcile unknown outcomes before retry. [ADR 019](../decisions/019-hob-backpack-and-continuations.md) sets the key scope, retention, lease and retry defaults.

Items for a cancelled or reassigned Task are recorded, checked against the Task's current owner and version, and resolved as late. They never restart work.

## The dispatcher

One dispatcher starts every turn that you did not start with Send. It replaces the chat queue's own trigger and the approval wake.

- An item goes to the session that owns the Task its recipient holds it under: the nearest Task, from the item's own up, that the recipient owns. What a member reports about its own Task, its result, failure, checkpoints, progress and questions, is held under the Task above it. For the top Task that session is its thread, where its owner chats with you; an item outside any Task goes to its thread. A Task's own item, and anything held under a step or a member's Task, goes to the session that works that Task: the step runner starts a step from its Task item, and a member's Task continues its member's own session.
- A live turn waiting for the item, such as a tool call waiting for its approval, gets it in place.
- An idle target gets one continuation that carries every pending actionable item, with the routine digest.
- Queued chat messages stay messages and start first; pending items ride along in the same turn.
- A turn starts only when the hob is active, within its limits and budget, and still owns or leads the Task. A paused hob keeps its items. A retired hob's items fail with a next action for its lead.

An ended parent turn does not discard the continuation obligation. A continuation's prompt is written by Hobbots and is never shown as a message from you: its reply follows the thread's last message with no message of yours before it.

How it runs today ([#292](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/292)):

- **Triggers.** A thread is woken after a commit that records an actionable item for it or sends one back to pending, when its turn ends, when its hob wakes, and at start. The announcement only wakes it; the stored items decide what runs. A wake that finds the thread busy is remembered, and so is a message you send while it is busy, and the thread looks at its items again once it is free, so work that arrives just after the dispatcher looked does not wait for another trigger.
- **One turn per wake.** The chat keeps the thread busy while the dispatcher claims every pending actionable item for it and puts them into one turn. Your queued message starts first and the items ride along as context; with none queued, the turn is a continuation whose prompt lists them. The turn holds the items' claims, so its `complete_task` consumes them; otherwise they resolve as consumed when the turn completes.
- **Ended early.** A turn that fails, is interrupted or is cut off sends its items back to pending and the dispatcher plans the turn again with backoff, until the delivery limit fails them. A turn whose claim is lost is stopped with its dispatch, so no second turn starts over it. A turn you stopped holds its items back until you write in that thread again, the hob wakes, or the hub restarts, so stopping it does not start it again on its own; your message then carries them, starting through the dispatcher under their claims even when the thread is idle. A stopped turn still counts as a delivery, so stopping the same work three times fails it as a blocker.
- **Gates.** A paused hob's items stay pending and its thread starts nothing until it wakes. An archived thread starts nothing until it is restored. A retired hob's items fail as blockers: when it retires, when one reaches it later or comes back to pending as the turn that held it ends, and at start.
- **Live turns.** A tool call waiting for its approval still gets the decision in place through the approval waiter, which is this live-turn branch. A request whose turn is gone, because Hobbots restarted, expires at start and its outcome becomes an Approval outcome item, so the hob hears that the call never ran.
- **Routine progress.** Progress for a thread's hob starts no turn and wakes nothing, however much piles up (IN-005). The screens follow each update as it is journaled. The thread's next turn carries every progress item waiting for it, under the same claims as the rest of its work: a continuation that an actionable item starts, or your next message, which then starts through the dispatcher. That turn is told of them as one digest after the work that needs the hob, a line per Task with how many updates it sent and the latest, and the items resolve as consumed with the turn. A turn that ends early sends its progress back to pending. The retry that its work needing the hob plans carries the progress again, but progress alone plans no retry and waits for the next turn, and it never runs out of deliveries. Stopping a turn that carried only progress holds nothing back. Progress held under a step or a member's Task is not carried yet: those sessions take only the work that needs them.
- **Members' Tasks** ([#273](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/273)). The same wake also starts the runner of members' Tasks. What a member holds under a Task another hob gave it, such as the Task itself, a hand-off, an answer or a result of its own steps, goes into one turn of the session that works that Task, each turn an Attempt of it, under the same claims, retries and gates as a thread's. An item whose Task has ended is still claimed there, so it resolves as late. An approval request from a member's turn names its Task, so an outcome no turn waits for goes back to that Task's session, not to the lead's thread. See the [orchestration contract](orchestration.md).
- **Not yet.** Deadlines that pass have no source yet, and budget and concurrency gates beyond the hob's status come with the limits that need them.

## Blockers

An item that fails becomes a **blocker** with a next action (IN-007), so no work silently disappears ([#296](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/296)).

- **Why.** Only actionable items become blockers: routine progress never needs your choice. Its deliveries ran out, every turn ending early (`retries-exhausted`, next action Retry), or its hob retired, so nobody can take it (`recipient-retired`, next action Dismiss). The blocker holds codes and counts, never display text (STD-I18N-005): its reason, its next action, how many deliveries the item had used, how its last try ended as `lastEnd` (`not-accepted`, `unavailable`, `stopped`, `interrupted`, `failed` or `limited`), and as `diagnostic` the error that try recorded, kept raw, or null when it recorded none: a turn you stopped records none. The web client writes the explanation from these. The item keeps every delivery and every blocker.
- **Where.** `GET /api/blockers` lists the items whose blocker waits for you, oldest first, with a stream cursor; the web client shows them on the Task card they are about and in Inbox › Needs you, and follows them through `BackpackItemRecorded` and `BackpackItemChanged`. Needs you says nothing needs you only once both approvals and blockers are read: while blockers load it says so, and a failed read says so with Try again.
- **Where it lives.** The choice and failing a retired hob's work are use cases in `@hobbots/inbox` (`decideBlocker`, `blockRetiredHob`, `blockIfRecipientRetired`, `blockRetiredWork`) behind the `BlockersHost` port; the hub maps their errors to HTTP and wires them to its event bus.
- **Choices.** `POST /api/blockers/:itemId/decision` with the number of the blocker you saw and `retry` or `dismiss`, from a user session only. The choice binds to that blocker: once the item failed again, a choice made for the earlier blocker is refused (`409 BlockerClosed`) and never reaches the newer one. Retry puts the item back to pending with as many deliveries again, and wakes its thread even where you had stopped work there, which lets the rest of the work you stopped in that thread start too; it is refused while the hob is retired. Dismiss resolves it as dismissed by you. The first choice wins, the blocker keeps it with its time, and the same choice again for the same blocker returns the item unchanged, without another round of deliveries. Reassign is offered but disabled until a Task can change owners ([#311](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/311)).
- **Ledger.** A failed item no longer holds its Task open, so a Task can complete while its blocker waits; a later Retry then resolves the item as late.
- **Not yet.** A retired member's lead does not get a Failure item for it, since members have no sessions of their own yet ([#273](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/273)). Missing authority and an unavailable runtime do not raise blockers yet.

## Responsibility ledger

The responsibility ledger is a read over the Task tree and the backpack, not a separate store. For a Task it lists, in this order: Tasks under it that have not ended, its wait while the wait still holds, sibling Tasks it comes after that have not completed, unanswered questions on it or under it, the milestones it promised and has not reached, approval requests still pending and unexpired in its thread (for the request's top Task, which chat turns work on) or asked by its member's turns (for a member's Task), and its owner's actionable backpack items about it or a Task under it that are not yet resolved or failed. Milestones are the promised outputs the hub can check; a Delegation's expected output is free text for the member and is not checked. A question shows once, as its Question record, not again as the lead's backpack item. Once a Task under it has ended, only that Task's result or failure still has to be read; its Task item, hand-offs, answers and reports have nothing left to act on, and completing the Task resolves those still pending and unclaimed as `late`. Routine progress never blocks. A supervisor can detect unfinished work without asking the model for status: `GET /api/tasks/:taskId/ledger` reads it. The completion gate and Quest closure use the same query. The gate never starts work: it refuses, and the refusal names the first eight open entries, in the order above, and counts the rest, so the hob can act on them in the same turn and the full list stays one ledger read away. A step whose final reply cannot complete it fails with a blocker checkpoint that says what was left open.

The gate leaves out the items the completing turn carries, and a successful completion resolves them as `consumed` in the same transaction. A carried item counts only while the turn's claim on it holds, by owner, fencing token and lease, or, for an item the turn was briefed with before any worker claimed it, while it is still pending, unclaimed and at the fencing token the turn saw. A continuation carries the items the dispatcher put into it, under its claims. A step's session carries its own Task item and the hand-offs its prompt shows, and a chat turn also carries what its briefing shows: the results and failures of the Tasks under its Task, and under its own steps at any depth, since those come back to the same hob, and the answers to the questions its hob asked about it. A turn carries an item only when the tree its prompt was made from shows the record at the version the item was recorded from or later, and only hand-offs that tree holds, so an item that arrives between reading the tree and reading the ledger is never consumed unread. Any other item, such as one that arrived during the turn, still blocks until a later turn carries it.

## User visibility

The web client shows what hobs are waiting on in three views of one list ([#297](https://github.com/otaviobarros/hobbots/issues/297)): the open hob's backpack under its row in the hob panel, the items about one request's Tasks in that request's section of the Quest tab, whichever hob's backpack they are in, and every hob's at the end of the Quest tab. The client reads `GET /api/backpack` once and keeps it current from `BackpackItemRecorded` and `BackpackItemChanged`; each view filters it, so an item moves in every view at once. Each item shows what it is and who sent it, the hob that owes a response and since when, and its state: Waiting (pending), Delivered, Acknowledged, Reading (processing), Stuck (failed, from the blocker list), or how it was resolved (Consumed, Answered, Late, Dismissed). A resolved item stays in view for a minute after it was handled, so you see it leave. Routine progress is a count that rides along the next turn, never a row. The actions are opening the item's Task in the drawer beside the chat it started in, and Retry or Dismiss once it is stuck. A member's finished result therefore shows as awaiting its lead from the moment it is recorded until the lead's continuation consumes it.

Verify result arrival while busy, inactive parent sessions, crash after commit and before delivery, duplicate delivery, out-of-order events, expired processing lease, exhausted retries and disabled recipients. No case may silently discard responsibility.
